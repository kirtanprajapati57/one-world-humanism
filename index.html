01 / Philosophy

Humanity before division.

Before we are citizens of nations, members of communities, or followers of traditions, we are human beings.


One-World Humanism imagines a future where humanity gradually moves beyond unnecessary divisions and builds a civilization centered on human dignity, freedom and progress.

“One humanity.
One world.
Freedom for every individual.
Welfare of all humanity.”

03 / Our Path

From nations toward humanity.

One-World Humanism does not demand that the world change overnight. The transformation is envisioned as a gradual process.

Today's Nations

Independent nations continue to exist while protecting the rights and dignity of their people.

Global Cooperation

Nations increasingly cooperate on problems that affect all humanity: peace, climate, health, science and technology.

Shared Institutions

Stronger international institutions develop to address problems that no individual nation can solve alone.

Greater Freedom of Movement

People gradually gain greater freedom to travel, study, work and live across borders.

Global Federation

Nations increasingly cooperate within a democratic global framework while preserving cultural diversity.

One Human Civilization

Humanity increasingly understands itself not as isolated groups, but as one interconnected civilization.

04 / The Manifesto

The One-World Humanist Manifesto

We do not promise paradise. We seek to create a better world—step by step, generation by generation.

I. One Humanity

There is only one human family.

No human being is inherently superior or inferior because of their birth, nationality, caste, wealth, religion, language, gender or social background.

Human dignity is universal.

II. Freedom of the Individual

Every person should have the freedom to shape their own life.

  • Choose their education and career.
  • Express their ideas.
  • Explore knowledge.
  • Pursue their ambitions.
  • Create, invent and experiment.
  • Form their own worldview.
  • Live according to their conscience.

But freedom carries responsibility.

“My freedom ends where another person's equal freedom begins.”

III. A World Without Unnecessary Borders

Borders are historical and political creations; humanity itself has no natural borders.

We envision a future in which people can increasingly move, study, work, collaborate and live across the world without being unnecessarily restricted by nationality.

International cooperation → easier movement → shared institutions → global federation → greater political unity.

IV. Peace Above War

War should never be humanity's first answer.

Nations should increasingly resolve disputes through diplomacy, international law, negotiation, institutions and peaceful cooperation.

The greatest victory is preventing humans from becoming enemies in the first place.

V. Equality of Human Dignity

Equality does not necessarily mean that every human being will have the same abilities, interests, achievements or possessions.

It means that every human being deserves equal fundamental dignity and rights.

Differences between people should enrich civilization— not divide humanity into superior and inferior groups.

VI. The End of Extreme Poverty

A civilization capable of reaching the stars should not accept a world where people are unable to meet their most basic needs.

  • Food
  • Clean water
  • Shelter
  • Education
  • Essential healthcare
  • Safety
  • The opportunity to build a meaningful life

We do not demand that everyone possess the same amount of wealth. We demand that poverty should never destroy a person's basic human potential.

VII. Education as a Human Right

Education should not exist merely to produce workers. Its deeper purpose is to produce thinking human beings.

  • Think critically.
  • Question assumptions.
  • Understand evidence.
  • Communicate.
  • Create.
  • Discover.
  • Cooperate.
  • Continuously learn.
A society that teaches people how to think can continuously progress.

VIII. Science, Reason & Knowledge

Humanity must remain willing to question itself.

Evidence over superstition.
Reason over dogma.
Curiosity over ignorance.
Discovery over fear.

Scientific power must be accompanied by ethical responsibility.

IX. Humanity & The Planet

Earth is the common home of humanity.

We seek a civilization capable of combining:

Economic development + technological progress + environmental responsibility.

Humanity should not be forced to choose between progress and nature.

X. Economic Freedom & Human Welfare

One-World Humanism does not define itself as exclusively capitalist or socialist.

We judge economic systems by their outcomes for human beings.

  • Innovation
  • Entrepreneurship
  • Productive competition
  • Individual initiative
  • Economic opportunity
  • Creation of wealth
Wealth should be a tool for human flourishing— not a measure of human worth.

XI. Democracy & Human Rights

Power must ultimately remain accountable to people. Government exists to serve human beings—not the other way around.

  • Freedom of thought
  • Freedom of expression
  • Equality before the law
  • Human rights
  • Due process
  • Political participation
  • Accountability

No government, institution, ideology or majority should possess unlimited power over the individual.

XII. The Path Toward World Unity

We do not seek to erase cultures.

Languages, traditions, art, philosophies, cuisines, histories and cultures can continue to flourish.

Unity does not require uniformity.

Different people can live together as equal members of one human civilization.

XIII. Human Advancement

The purpose of civilization should be more than survival.

Humanity should seek to understand more, create more, discover more and become capable of more.

  • Science
  • Medicine
  • Technology
  • Education
  • Space exploration
  • Artificial intelligence
  • Energy
  • Culture
  • Philosophy
  • Human cooperation

XIV. The Individual & Humanity

We reject the idea that the individual and humanity must always stand against one another.

The individual gives humanity creativity.
Humanity gives the individual opportunity.

We therefore seek a balance between individual freedom and social responsibility.

XV. Our Long-Term Vision

Our ultimate aspiration is a civilization in which:

  • No person is considered inferior because of their birth.
  • No child is denied opportunity because of birthplace.
  • No human being is forced into extreme poverty.
  • War is no longer an ordinary instrument of politics.
  • People can move across the world with fewer artificial barriers.
  • Knowledge is widely accessible.
  • Science and reason guide our understanding.
  • Technology expands human possibility.
  • Nature is protected for future generations.
  • Governments exist to protect human rights and freedom.

Humanity increasingly sees itself not as billions of separate groups—but as one civilization.

05 / The Ultimate Vision

Beyond unnecessary division.

We do not promise paradise. We do not claim that human conflict can disappear completely. We do not believe that humanity will become perfect.

We believe something simpler:

Humanity can become better.
Final Declaration

You are human.
I am human.

We share one world.

Let every generation leave humanity with more knowledge, more freedom, greater dignity and greater possibilities than the generation before it.
